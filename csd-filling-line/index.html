Carbonated Soft Drink Filling Lines

Isobaric filling lines for CSD, sparkling water and energy drinks

Carbonated products are filled by counter-pressure: the bottle is pre-pressurized with CO₂ so the liquid does not foam and lose carbonation. Everything else in a CSD line — chilling, filling valve design, capping — exists to protect that principle.

Line configuration

A CSD line runs: water treatment and syrup blending, carbonation (mixer), bottle rinsing, isobaric filler-capper, warmer (for chilled filling), labeler and coder, then packing. The mixer and the filler are matched as one system — a mixer sized beyond the filler's capability wastes CO₂ and chills the line.

Isobaric filling in practice

The filler pressurizes each bottle with CO₂ to the tank pressure before opening the filling valve, so liquid flows without turbulence. Filling temperature and pressure are paired: chilled product at low temperature holds carbonation with lower pressure; warm filling needs higher pressure and a warmer tunnel after capping to prevent condensation before labeling.

Capacity selection

Match the mixer, filler and warmer as one system. A line sized 3,000–9,000 BPH covers most regional CSD brands; the mixer's carbonation capacity (grams per liter at the required temperature) decides the ceiling more than the filler does.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is counter-pressure (isobaric) filling?

The bottle is first pressurized with CO₂ to match the filling tank pressure, then the valve opens and liquid flows by gravity without foaming. Without counter-pressure, carbonation escapes as foam and fill levels become inconsistent.

Can a PET line fill carbonated drinks?

Yes. PET bottles for CSD use a neck finish and panel design rated for the carbonation level; the filler uses the same isobaric principle. Verify the bottle specification against your CO₂ volumes before ordering.

Why does the line need a warmer after filling?

Chilled bottles coming out of the filler condense moisture, which interferes with labeling. A warm tunnel raises the bottle surface above dew point so labels apply correctly.

How is foam controlled during filling?

By pairing filling pressure and temperature, pre-pressurizing the bottle, and using filling valves with a slow-start profile. Product formulation also matters — sugar level and temperature both affect foaming.
