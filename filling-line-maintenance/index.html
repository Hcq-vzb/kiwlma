Filling Line Maintenance Guide

Preventive schedules, spare parts strategy and fault diagnosis

Most filling line downtime is not machine failure — it is seal wear, cap-chute jams and conveyor guide misalignment that a preventive schedule would have caught. This guide sets out the checks, the spares to stock, and the faults behind the most common symptoms.

Daily, weekly and monthly checks

IntervalCheckWhy
Every shiftFill levels, cap torque sample, conveyor guide alignment, air pressure Catches drift before it becomes scrap
WeeklyNozzle O-rings and check valves, cap chute wear pads, vacuum pump condition These are the parts that wear fastest in liquid service
MonthlySeamer/ capper head teardown check, valve diaphragms, drive belt tension, sensor calibration Prevents unplanned stoppages at the two highest-load stations
QuarterlyFull valve rebuild, seal kit replacement, safety interlock test Restores the machine to baseline before wear compounds

Spare parts stocking strategy

Stock wear parts (O-rings, diaphragms, nozzle seals, cap chute pads, belts) in one shift's quantity; stock critical spares (filler valve cores, capper heads, PLC I/O modules) at one set per machine. Everything else is ordered against the machine serial number — ordering by photograph or memory is how wrong parts arrive.

Common symptoms and their actual causes

SymptomFirst checks
Dripping nozzles after fillNozzle check valve spring, fill cutoff timing, product temperature
Cap jams in the chuteChute wear pads, cap orientation, air blow pressure
Fill level varianceSupply pressure stability, valve diaphragm wear, bottle neck quality
Label misalignmentProduct detection sensor timing, label reel tension, bottle plate wear
Bottles falling at transferStar wheel alignment, guide rail gaps, conveyor speed mismatch

Changeover discipline

Most damage happens during format changeovers, not production. A written changeover checklist — parts removed, parts reinstalled, torque values, first-article check — prevents the classic failure of running a new bottle format with the previous format's guide rails.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which filling line parts fail first?

Nozzle O-rings and check valves, cap chute wear pads, valve diaphragms and conveyor belts — all are wear parts in liquid service and should be stocked on site rather than ordered after failure.

How often should a filling line be serviced?

Daily operational checks each shift, weekly checks of nozzle and cap-path wear parts, monthly checks of the high-load stations (filler valves, capper heads), and a quarterly rebuild of seals and wear kits.

What causes dripping nozzles after filling?

Usually the nozzle check valve spring or fill cutoff timing; product temperature affects viscosity and can also cause dripping after the valve closes.

How do I know which spare parts to stock?

Stock wear parts in one-shift quantities and critical spares (valve cores, capper heads, PLC modules) at one set per machine, ordered against the machine serial number.
